PROJECT 01 / ROBOTICS · 2023

Wearable Walking
Robot Prototype

Harvard PHYS S-12 · Independent project

An exploration of assistive mobility through custom mechanical linkages, 3D-printed components, and coordinated motor control.

The challenge

I independently designed and fabricated a robotic walking mechanism using Fusion 360, 3D printing, and four servo motors. My initial stepper-motor design produced only about 30° of rotation instead of the intended 180°. After experimenting with a gearbox, I redesigned the mechanism with servos to control the hip and knee joints.

How I approached it

I studied photographs and videos of human gait, identified four phases of walking, and measured hip and knee angles to establish target positions. I programmed the four servos in Arduino, accounting for opposite mounting orientations and staggering their movement into alternating leg cycles.

What I learned

The completed physical prototype demonstrated the integration of mechanical design, additive manufacturing, electronics, and motion control. Troubleshooting motor limitations taught me to revise the mechanism rather than force an unsuitable early design.

PROJECT 02 / AEROSPACE · 2026

Vanderbilt
Rocket Team

Carbon-fiber manufacturing · CAD · Composite materials

Hands-on airframe fabrication, with materials research to support epoxy selection and testing.

Manufacturing

I worked with teammates to manufacture carbon-fiber tubes for a rocket airframe, participating in layup, epoxy application, curing, and finishing. We applied carbon fiber around cylindrical molds and prepared components for further assembly.

Materials research

I researched epoxy options, strength, application, curing methods, and the loads bonded components could support. A teammate carried out the physical epoxy testing; my contribution was the supporting research.

Outcome

I contributed to carbon-fiber tube fabrication and epoxy-testing research, developing practical experience with composite construction and collaboration on a larger engineering project.

PROJECT 03 / ARCHITECTURE · 2022

Mexico City
Public Library

Oxford Summer Courses · Independent conceptual design

A nature-inspired, nine-story public library envisioned as a welcoming place to read, study, and explore.

The idea

For a site in Mexico City, I chose to design a public library that would benefit the neighborhood. Architecture by Gaudí and Singapore's Supertree Grove inspired the curved exterior, greenery, and open spaces.

The design

My nine-floor proposal included a central spiral staircase, study and reading areas, natural lighting, solar panels, and planted walkways. I made hand drawings and a physical scale model to develop the concept.

Site & community

I researched nearby museums, apartments, businesses, and pedestrian access to understand how the building could serve its surroundings. I wanted natural elements to be integrated into the architecture, rather than added as decoration.

PROJECT 04 / STRUCTURES · FALL 2025

Steel Truss
Design & Testing

Vanderbilt CE2200 Statics · Team project

A physical welded truss informed by structural calculations and a community-centered proposal for Nashville.

Design & analysis

Our team designed a truss representing a proposed cap over I-40 in Nashville. We used the methods of joints and sections to determine internal forces for a 350 lb design load, checked calculations with the Johns Hopkins Truss Calculator, and estimated buckling and yielding limits.

Fabrication & testing

We assembled pre-cut steel members with connector plates, tack-welded the connections, and reinforced welds as needed. The first visible buckling occurred around 150 lb; the truss ultimately supported approximately 400 lb before significant deformation.

Community context & results

The highway-cap concept explored reconnection of the Jefferson Street neighborhood through green space, a community building, and a playground. Physical testing confirmed buckling as the main failure mode, although it began earlier than the idealized model predicted.
